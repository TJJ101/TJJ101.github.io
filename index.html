Lightweight Image-Space Deformation

C++, GLSL, OpenGL, CMake, ImGui

A computer graphics research project on simulating surface bending, compression and wrinkles in a fragment shader, without changing the mesh.

Research poster submitted to Pacific Graphics 2026

The Idea

A computer graphics research project on simulating convincing surface deformation without modifying the underlying mesh geometry. Instead of traditional geometry-based deformation, it uses height information and fragment-shader techniques to make surfaces look like they're bending, compressing, and forming wrinkles.

How It Works

The implementation uses a height-guided parallax approach. Rather than physically moving vertices or introducing a skeletal rig, the fragment shader perturbs texture coordinates based on height information to simulate changes in surface depth.

Deformation is generated at the image level while keeping the original mesh intact, reducing the need for expensive geometry updates.

Materials and Wrinkles

The system is designed to support different materials and deformation characteristics. Various models and height maps are used to experiment with how surfaces respond to interaction:

  • Skin-like surfaces
  • Gel-like surfaces
  • Softer materials

Particular attention goes to wrinkles and folds, where the visual response should reflect the direction and intensity of the applied deformation.

Haptic Interaction (WIP)

A physical input device with a pincer-style mechanism provides a tangible way to interact with the simulated surface. It connects physical input with the visual deformation response, exploring how image-space techniques can be extended beyond purely visual applications.

Get Me Out of Hell

C++, GLSL, OpenGL ES 3.0, Android Studio

A 2D roguelite where terrain, materials and hazards shape every decision. Built on a custom framework for Windows and Android.

Selected for a public showcase at SIT Artistry 2026

Overview

Get Me Out of Hell is a creepy-cute 2D roguelike dungeon crawler inspired by the Moon Rabbit folklore of East Asian mythology. While drawing inspiration from traditional dungeon-crawling experiences, the game introduces a different approach to dungeon progression: instead of exploring a pre-built dungeon, players construct their own dungeon one tile at a time, shaping the challenges they will encounter as they progress.

Dungeon Building and Gameplay Loop

At the beginning of a run, players enter a dedicated map-building room where they can place a new dungeon tile. Each tile must be connected to the starting tile, allowing players to gradually expand their dungeon while determining its layout.

Every newly placed tile is generated using a combination of randomly selected parameters, including its shape, modifiers, and loot rarity. These elements introduce variation between tiles, giving each placement the potential to create a different encounter and reward opportunity.

Once a tile has been placed, players enter the dungeon and must clear that tile before returning to the map-building room. Only after completing the encounter can they expand their dungeon by placing another tile. This creates the game's core gameplay loop:

  • Map Building
  • Dungeon Combat
  • Map Building
  • Dungeon Combat
  • Repeat

By alternating between dungeon construction and combat, the game gives players an active role in shaping their own progression. Dungeon building becomes more than just a preparation phase; it directly determines the spaces players will explore and the challenges they will face.

Technical Implementation

Developed using C++ and OpenGL ES 3.0, the project was built using a custom engine rather than relying on a full-fledged commercial game engine. The development process involved implementing the underlying systems needed to support the game's 2D gameplay and rendering. The project was also developed for Windows and Android, introducing additional considerations for cross-platform compatibility.

Dwarf Mayhem

C++, Alpha Engine, Cellular Automata

A Noita-inspired 2D game where a cellular-automata material simulation drives destructible terrain.

Overview

A Noita-inspired 2D mining game that explores simulation-driven gameplay through cellular automata and destructible environments. Players dig down toward the core of a world where materials interact dynamically instead of staying fixed in a predefined level.

Gameplay Loop

  1. Mine your way down to the core
  2. Sell what you collect at the shop. Every mined material earns money, and the shop only opens near the surface.
  3. Upgrade your gear with the money.
  4. Dig Deeper and repeat.

Ores are stone that sells for more money, so finding them speeds up progress.

The Deeper You Go, The Harder It Gets

  • Heat: temperature rises with depth, and players take more and more damage. Surviving means buying more heat armour and moving faster.
  • Limited storage: players can only carry so much, and can upgrade their storage at the shop.

Because the shop only opens near the surface and storage is limited, players have to decide how deep to push before heading back to sell.

Materials

Every material in the world is simulated with cellular automata, and falls into one of three groups:

  • Solids: Sand, Stone, Dirt (plus Ores, which are Stone that sells for more)
  • Liquids: Water, Lava
  • Gases: Steam, Toxic Gas

How Each Material Behaves

  • Liquids flow down.
  • Stone stays in place. Sand and dirt behave like their real-life counterparts: they fall and flow, but don’t spread out like a liquid.
  • Gases expand and float up.

Liquids and gases also have a certain compressibility.

Material Interactions

  • Water meets lava and creates stone and steam.
  • Solids sink through liquids, displacing the liquid.
  • Gases rise through liquids.

Rather than relying on manually scripted interactions alone, each material element updates according to its surrounding conditions, so the state of the environment keeps changing as the player interacts with it.

Destructible Terrain

The environment is not just a set of static obstacles. It can be altered during gameplay, letting players affect the world around them. Mining is how players earn money, so destroying terrain is the core of the game, and it opens up emergent interactions along the way.

Technical Implementation

Built in C++ using the Alpha Engine. The project involved implementing simulation-based systems in a 2D game, using cellular automata to represent dynamic materials and connecting those systems to the gameplay experience.
